## Main Figures

### Figure 1. XevaDB integrates longitudinal PDX pharmacogenomic data into a machine-learning-ready resource.

**(a)** Curation workflow: raw longitudinal drug-response, omics, and clinical-annotation data from four PDX cohorts (McGill Breast, UHN Breast, UHN Lung, PDXE pan-cancer) are standardized (including drug and gene annotation) into harmonized XevaSet objects and integrated into XevaDB. **(b)** XevaDB's integrated XevaSet data structure — linking model metadata, dataset information, omics profiles, drug annotation, omics-response mapping, longitudinal response, replicate design, and response metrics — compared with the fragmented, model-centric, summary-level records typical of conventional PDX resources. **(c)** Four case studies built on XevaDB, each pairing a specific XevaDB data layer with a machine-learning approach: (1) growth-kinetics modeling of intrinsic PDX proliferation from control-arm curves and RNA-seq; (2) CNN-based classification of drug-sensitivity from standardized tumor-volume-curve images; (3) transfer-learning assessment of a cell-line-trained model (NeST-VNN) on PDX response, mutation, and copy-number data; (4) multi-omics fusion-model benchmarking for paclitaxel response prediction.

---

### Figure 2. Transcriptomic modeling of intrinsic PDX growth kinetics.

**(a)** Analysis workflow: control-arm longitudinal tumor-volume curves and matched RNA-seq from breast and lung PDX cohorts are used to estimate model-level doubling time, which is then modeled by ElasticNet, Random Forest, and Lassoed Forest classifiers under repeated cross-validation. **(b)** Representative doubling-time estimation for one control-arm mouse: raw tumor-volume trajectory (top; points within the 35-day fitting window in blue, excluded points in gray) and the corresponding exponential-growth fit (ln(tumor volume) vs. time, bottom), with goodness-of-fit (R²) and doubling time (DT) annotated. **(c)** Distribution of estimated doubling times across breast (n = 75) and lung (n = 27) PDX models, with the fastest- and slowest-growing tertiles used to define the classification task highlighted (breast: n = 25/25; lung: n = 9/9). **(d)** Cross-validated AUROC of ElasticNet, Random Forest, and Lassoed Forest classifiers distinguishing fast- from slow-growing models, across all completed outer folds (breast: 50; lung: 28); dashed line, chance performance. **(e)** Concordance between ElasticNet standardized coefficients and Random Forest mean |SHAP| values for genes associated with fast growth (top) and slow growth (bottom) in the breast cohort (Pearson r annotated), with representative top genes labeled. **(f)** Overlap of predictive genes (≥50% fold-selection frequency) between breast and lung cohorts (Venn diagram) and mean |SHAP| importance of the top shared and cohort-specific genes, stratified by fast- (solid) and slow-growth (dotted) association; genes selected in both cohorts are shown in bold, with per-cohort selection frequency (% of folds) indicated. **(g)** Cross-cohort comparison of Hallmark gene-set enrichment (GSEA normalized enrichment score, NES) associated with fast- vs. slow-growing tumors in the breast and lung cohorts.

### Figure 3. Image-based deep learning captures non-linear drug-response dynamics.

**(a)** Analysis workflow: tumor-volume growth curves for model-drug batches from the PDXE pan-cancer cohort are rendered as standardized images, independently scored by a three-rater expert panel into five ordinal response categories, and used to train and compare an image-based ResNet18 classifier against a feature-based Random Forest classifier under matched 5-fold cross-validation. **(b)** Number of curves assigned to each of the five response categories (Cat_1, Highly Resistant; Cat_2, Resistant; Cat_3, Intermediate; Cat_4, Sensitive; Cat_5, Highly Sensitive) by each of the three individual raters and by consensus (n = 1,267 consensus-labeled curves). **(c)** Representative ResNet18 training trajectory for one cross-validation fold, showing training/validation cross-entropy loss (left axis) and validation balanced accuracy (right axis) across epochs; star, checkpoint selected by early stopping. **(d)** Cross-validated performance (macro-averaged precision, recall, F1-score, and balanced accuracy) of ResNet18 versus Random Forest across 5 outer folds. **(e)** Pooled out-of-fold confusion matrices for ResNet18 and Random Forest across all 1,267 curves, each predicted by the model that had not seen it during training. **(f)** Random Forest feature importance (mean |SHAP| value, stacked by response category) for the fourteen engineered response features used for classification. **(g)** Representative Grad-CAM visualizations for one correctly classified, genuinely held-out example per response category, showing the curve regions driving the ResNet18 classification.

### Figure 4. Cross-platform validation of NeST-VNN on independent PDX cohorts.

**(a)** Analysis workflow: drug-response, mutation, and copy-number data from the McGill (cisplatin, n = 29) and UHN (carboplatin; complete cohort n = 68, replicate-supported "strict" cohort n = 36) breast PDX cohorts are used as external input to a cell-line-pretrained NeST-VNN ensemble, and predicted response is validated against a growth-curve-slope-angle response label. **(b)** Fraction of the 718-gene NeST-VNN input panel with recorded mutation and copy-number (CNV) calls in each cohort's raw molecular profiles. **(c)** Per-model number of nonzero mutation, copy-number amplification (CNA), and copy-number deletion (CND) events in the McGill and UHN cohorts (two-sided Mann–Whitney U test; \*\*\*\*, p < 0.0001; ns, not significant). **(d)** Number of models with exactly one versus two or more treatment-arm replicate mice, for each cohort/cohort definition. **(e)** Distribution of treatment-arm growth-curve slope angle in the McGill and UHN cohorts, with the fixed 0° cutoff used to define responding (slope < 0°) versus non-responding (slope ≥ 0°) models. **(f)** Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for NeST-VNN's AUROC in predicting the slope-angle response label, for each of the three cohort definitions; dashed line, chance performance. **(g)** Receiver operating characteristic curves for NeST-VNN's predicted response versus the slope-angle response label in each cohort, with AUROC annotated.

### Figure 5. Multi-omics prediction of paclitaxel response.

**(a)** Analysis workflow: paclitaxel drug-sensitivity data and RNA-seq, CNV, and mutation profiles from the UHN breast PDX cohort are integrated into single-omics, early-fusion, and late-fusion classification models, benchmarked by cross-validated performance. **(b)** Distribution of batch-level growth-curve slope angle across the 80-batch paclitaxel cohort, colored by the binary response label (Resistant/Sensitive) defined by a fixed median split (median indicated). **(c)** Agreement between the mRECIST-based response category (PD/SD/PR/CR) and the binary angle-median-split response label used for downstream modeling (overall agreement, 76.2%). **(d)** Cross-validated AUROC (25 repeated cross-validation folds) for single-omics (RNA, CNV, mutation), early-fusion, and late-fusion classifiers; dashed line, chance performance. **(e)** Pre-ranked GSEA normalized enrichment scores (NES) for pathways associated with Resistant (negative NES) versus Sensitive (positive NES) models, across the GO Biological Process, KEGG, and MSigDB Hallmark gene-set collections.

## Supplementary Figures

**Supplementary Figure 1. Pooled ROC curves for growth-kinetics classifiers.** Receiver operating characteristic curves (with bootstrap 95% confidence bands) for ElasticNet, Random Forest, and Lassoed Forest classifiers distinguishing fast- from slow-growing PDX models in the breast and lung cohorts; mean AUROC is annotated for each classifier. Related to Fig. 2d.

**Supplementary Figure 2. ResNet18 training curves across cross-validation folds.** Training/validation cross-entropy loss (left axis) and validation balanced accuracy (right axis) across epochs for each of the five outer cross-validation folds (Fold 0–4) used in the image-based drug-response classification; the checkpoint with the best validation balanced accuracy (star) in each fold was reloaded and used for evaluation on that fold's held-out test partition. Related to Fig. 3c.

**Supplementary Figure 3. Threshold-sensitivity analysis for NeST-VNN cross-cohort validation.** Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for AUROC as a function of the response-defining percentile cutoff on treatment-arm slope, swept from the 10th to 90th percentile of each cohort's own slope distribution, for the McGill cisplatin, UHN carboplatin complete, and UHN carboplatin strict cohorts; dashed line, chance performance. Related to Fig. 4f, g.

**Supplementary Figure 4. Sensitivity of doubling-time estimation to quality-control thresholds.** Number of mice and PDX models retained across a grid of candidate MIN_POINTS (minimum tumor-volume measurements per mouse) and MIN_R2 (minimum goodness-of-fit) thresholds, for the lung (top) and breast (bottom) cohorts; dotted lines indicate the thresholds used in the primary analysis (MIN_POINTS = 3, MIN_R2 = 0.8). Related to Fig. 2b, c.

**Supplementary Figure 5. Follow-up duration is confounded with estimated growth rate.** For the breast (top) and lung (bottom) cohorts: distribution of raw, uncensored follow-up duration per mouse (left); correlation between fitted growth-rate coefficient *k* and follow-up duration (two-sided Spearman r annotated; middle); and drift in median retained doubling time as a function of the candidate follow-up-window cutoff, MAX_DAY (right). Dotted line, the 35-day cutoff used in the primary analysis. Related to Fig. 2b, c.

**Supplementary Figure 6. Sensitivity of growth-kinetics classifiers to the number of highly variable genes.** Cross-validated AUROC (mean ± SD) for ElasticNet and Random Forest (top) and distribution of Lassoed Forest mean AUROC across the max_depth × min_samples_leaf hyperparameter grid (bottom), across a grid of candidate highly-variable-gene (HVG) counts, for the breast and lung cohorts. The HVG counts used in the primary analysis (N = 100 breast, N = 600 lung) were selected based on this comparison. Related to Fig. 2d.
